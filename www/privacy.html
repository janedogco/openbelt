Privacy

Updated November 17, 2026

What OpenBelt keeps

  • Your email address and the name your workspace shows for you.
  • Your sign-in sessions, in a cookie.
  • Your workspaces: their people, groups, roles and tokens.
  • Your apps: their files, versions and the data they store.
  • Connections to other services you add, with their sign-in tokens. Tokens and secrets are stored encrypted.
  • Usage: who opened which app on which day, kept 90 days. Each app keeps its last 500 log lines. Server logs and traces are kept 14 days.
  • Visits to the home, Start and sign-in pages, in the server logs. Each visitor is counted by an ID that changes every day, made from your network address and browser. The address itself isn't kept.
  • Which site sent you here (for example news.ycombinator.com), in a cookie, for 7 days. It holds no ID.
  • Backups of all of the above, kept up to 30 days.

What OpenBelt doesn't do

  • It doesn't sell your data or share it with advertisers.
  • It shows no ads and uses no tracking cookies.

Sign in with Google

When you continue with Google, OpenBelt asks Google only for your email address and that it is verified. It uses it to sign you in. It gets no access to your Gmail, Drive, contacts or anything else in your Google account.

Who else handles it

  • Vultr hosts OpenBelt's servers in the United States.
  • Cloudflare sends sign-in emails, stores backups and runs DNS.
  • Cloudflare Web Analytics counts visits to the home page and this page, without cookies.
  • The services you connect get what your apps and agents send them, as you set up.

Deleting your data

  • Delete an app from its ⋯ menu on your workspace's home page: its pages, data and files go for good.
  • A workspace admin can remove you from the workspace.
  • To delete your account or a whole workspace, open an issue on GitHub. Don't put private details in it; we'll reply there and confirm with you before deleting anything.
